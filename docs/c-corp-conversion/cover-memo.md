## What this package is

Four draft documents to support converting The Tobacco Center, LLC (a Pennsylvania limited liability company operating a retail cigar shop in Williamsport, PA) into a Pennsylvania business corporation, via Pennsylvania's statutory conversion procedure (15 Pa.C.S., Subchapter on entity conversions):

1. `plan-of-conversion.md` — the LLC members' approval document
2. `articles-of-incorporation.md` — forms the new corporation as part of the conversion filing
3. `bylaws.md` — initial governance rules for the corporation
4. `organizational-consent.md` — the new board's first action: adopts bylaws, issues stock, appoints officers, authorizes the bank account and EIN application

These were drafted from research into Pennsylvania's current entity-conversion statute and filing requirements, not from a review of the LLC's actual operating agreement, membership records, or prior tax filings. **Every bracketed placeholder needs to be filled in and the substance checked against the real facts before anything here is signed or filed.**

## The one decision that has to happen before any of this gets used

There are three different ways to get from "LLC" to "C-corp," and they are not interchangeable:

| | Form 8832 tax election | **This package: full statutory conversion** | New corp + contribution |
|---|---|---|---|
| Legal form | Stays an LLC | Becomes an actual PA corporation | Becomes an actual PA corporation |
| New EIN | No | Yes | Yes |
| Qualifies cleanly for QSBS (§1202) on an eventual sale | Disputed — LLC "membership interests" may not count as "stock" | Yes | Yes, and avoids certain look-through complications the conversion route can trigger |
| Re-licensing (PA tobacco license, bank, lease, merchant account) | Not needed | Required | Required |
| Effort | One IRS form | What this package covers | Similar effort to the conversion route, different mechanics |

**Please confirm which of these actually fits the client's goal before this package is used for anything.** If the goal is simply retained-earnings tax treatment or fringe-benefit deductibility with no plan to sell the business or bring in outside equity, Form 8832 alone may be suffound as sufficient and this entire package would be unnecessary. If a future sale and the QSBS exclusion are in play, the full conversion (or the new-corp-plus-contribution alternative) is the right call, and the specific mechanics should be chosen with QSBS basis/holding-period rules in mind.

## Specific things to verify or decide

- **Entity names**: confirm the LLC's exact legal name and PA entity (file) number, and confirm "The Tobacco Center, Inc." is available as the new corporate name (PA Dept. of State business name search).
- **Membership → stock conversion ratio**: list every current LLC member and ownership percentage; the Plan of Conversion and the Organizational Consent both need this to set share issuance.
- **Authorized shares / par value**: draft documents use 1,000 shares of common stock, no par value, as a placeholder. Confirm this doesn't create QSBS basis issues given the planned use case.
- **§351 qualification**: confirm the conversion structure results in the contributing members holding ≥80% control of the new corporation immediately after, so the asset transfer qualifies for tax-free treatment under IRC §351.
- **Registered office / agent**: needs an actual PA street address (no PO box) and a named registered agent.
- **Initial directors/officers**: placeholder assumes the current LLC members become the initial director(s); officer titles need to be assigned.
- **Regulated-entity approval**: Pennsylvania's Statement of Conversion form (DSCB:15-355) flags that a conversion involving a regulated entity may need a government agency's prior approval. Worth confirming directly with PA DOS whether the PA OTP tobacco license triggers this before filing.
- **Sequencing with the multi-state tobacco compliance work**: The Tobacco Center has a 43-state cigar-shipping registration plan in progress (separate project, see the Registration Playbook and Compliance Atlas in this repo). Several of those state filings require proof of an active PA tobacco license held by the filing entity. Recommend completing the entity conversion and re-licensing the PA tobacco permit under the new corporate name **before** starting those state filings, to avoid filing 43 states under the LLC's name and then redoing them.

## Filing mechanics already confirmed (as of this pull date)

- PA Statement of Conversion: Form DSCB:15-355, **$70** filing fee, filed with the PA Department of State, Bureau of Corporations and Charitable Organizations, via file.dos.pa.gov.
- Must be accompanied by Form DSCB:15-134B (Docketing Statement — Changes).
- PA Annual Report (replaces the old decennial report): Form DSCB:15-146, **$7**, due January 1–June 30 each year once incorporated; late filing risks administrative dissolution starting with the 2027 report cycle.
- PA corporate net income tax rate for 2026: **7.49%**, on a legislated phase-down to 4.99% by 2031 (Act 53 of 2022). Combined with the flat 21% federal corporate rate, that's roughly 28.5% at the corporate level in 2026 before any tax on distributed dividends — the double-taxation exposure to weigh against whatever specific benefit is driving this conversion.

Everything above should be independently verified against the PA Department of State and Department of Revenue directly before relying on it.
