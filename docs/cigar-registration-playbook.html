Before you file anywhere

Steps that apply regardless of which state you're registering in. Do these once, up front.

  1. Confirm your PA license is current and in good standing. Several states' applications (Maryland's Remote Tobacco Seller License, Tennessee's reciprocity requirement) explicitly ask for proof of an active home‑state tobacco license — have a copy ready to attach.
  2. Get a federal EIN if you don't already file under one, and register for federal PACT Act reporting with the ATF if you ever sell cigarettes, smokeless, RYO, or vapor products alongside cigars (cigars themselves are exempt from PACT Act registration).
  3. Check whether the state requires Secretary‑of‑State foreign‑entity registration before it will issue a tobacco license. A few states (Kansas, Massachusetts's nonresident‑distributor track, others) tie the tobacco application to appointing an in‑state agent for service of process — this is usually a separate, quick filing, but do it first so it doesn't hold up the tobacco license.
  4. Set up one age‑verification method that satisfies everyone. Nearly every state's delivery‑sale statute requires either (a) a commercial/independent third‑party age‑verification service at checkout, or (b) an adult (21+) signature required at delivery, or both. A single compliant checkout flow (e.g. an ID‑verification vendor) plus a carrier delivery option requiring adult signature covers the large majority of states in one setup.
  5. Set your shipping method accordingly. Cigars are exempt from the federal USPS non‑mailability rule, but several states still require a specific delivery method (signature‑required, ID‑checked) regardless of carrier — UPS and FedEx both offer adult‑signature‑required services that satisfy this in every state that asks for it.
  6. Decide whether to do this yourself or hand it to a service. With ~44 separate state filings, most retailers at this scale use a multi‑state tobacco licensing/compliance service (or a tobacco‑compliance attorney) rather than filing each one manually — worth pricing out before you start, especially for the states with bonds attached, since a compliance service can often bundle bonding.

Do not file in these states

Confirmed banned or barred — no license makes these shippable today
  • Vermont — 7 V.S.A. §1010 bans mail/phone/internet tobacco sales to consumers outright.
  • Washington — RCW 70.155.140 bans shipment of most tobacco products directly to consumers.
  • Utah — banned today (Utah Code §76‑10‑105.1); a licensed pathway opens January 1, 2027 — put this on your calendar, not your current filing list.
  • Arkansas — its online‑cigar‑sale rule requires both an Arkansas physical presence and sourcing from an AR‑permitted wholesaler; a remote‑only seller cannot qualify.
  • Iowa — Iowa Code Ch.453A prohibits delivery sales of cigars to consumers outright; only vapor/alternative‑nicotine products have a delivery‑sale permit pathway there.
Call before you file
  • New Hampshire — the tax exemption for premium cigars is solid, but the retail‑licensing statute runs on a different, broader "tobacco product" definition with no premium‑cigar carve‑out, and no out‑of‑state "remote seller" license option was found. Call NH DRA (603‑230‑4359) or the NH Liquor Commission to ask directly whether a no‑premises out‑of‑state retailer can be licensed, before filing anything.
