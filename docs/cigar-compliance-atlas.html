    Jurisdictions still needing follow‑up before any filing

    Most of the original priority list closed out in a 2026‑09‑28 verification pass — most notably Maryland, which moved from the single highest‑risk state in the first draft (taxed at what looked like 70%, DTC status unresolved) to one of the more favorable ones (confirmed 15% premium‑cigar rate, plus a dedicated $25/yr Remote Tobacco Seller License).     2. Minnesota — the claimed $0.65/cigar inflation‑adjusted cap could not be corroborated against any Minnesota‑domain source on a second dedicated search; rely on the statutory $0.50 cap and treat $0.65 as a likely aggregator artifact.

Maryland — the core legality question is now resolved favorably, but the Remote Tobacco Seller License's bond requirement (if any) and the practical mechanics of MD Workers' Compensation compliance for an out‑of‑state, no‑employee‑in‑MD business are unconfirmed. Contact ATCC (443‑300‑6990) directly.
    4. Delaware — HB 215's final floor passage and gubernatorial signature are unconfirmed (though the premium‑cigar rate is stable at 30% either way); confirm the license‑fee increase ($200→$400) before budgeting for DE entry.
    5. Connecticut — HB 7275 is still in committee, not enacted, but its current draft has no premium‑cigar exemption; monitor closely, as passage would flip CT to banned.
    6. Tennessee — SB 2086 is still pending, not enacted; monitor, as passage would flip TN to banned.
    7. Kansas — DTC legality is now well‑supported by the absence of any cigar‑specific restriction in K.S.A. 79‑3333, but this is an inference, not an affirmative statutory permission; get final confirmation from KS DOR (785‑368‑8222).

    Methodology & limitations

    Research method. All 49 jurisdictions were researched via automated web search against primary government sources (state Departments of Revenue/Taxation, Comptrollers, ABC/tobacco‑enforcement agencies), cross‑checked across multiple queries per state. In several sessions, direct fetching of the underlying .gov pages was blocked by network policy, so those findings rely on search‑engine synthesis of primary sources rather than a full direct read. A compliance reviewer should open every cited source directly before relying on this page operationally.

    Aggregator errors caught and corrected: a claim that "Georgia bans internet tobacco sales" traced to law governing the country of Georgia, not the U.S. state, and was discarded; a Minnesota cap figure ($0.65) traced to what appears to be an Ohio source and remains unconfirmed after a second dedicated check; a North Carolina $400 permit‑fee claim could not be corroborated against the statutory $25 figure and was discarded in its favor; a West Virginia "51%" and "7%" rate claim were both superseded by the confirmed current 12% statute text; a Maryland "70% flat rate" claim was corrected to a three‑tier structure (30% general OTP / 15% premium cigars / 70% non‑premium cigars) once the actual bulletin was reviewed directly.

    Second‑pass verification (2026‑09‑28): the priority follow‑up list from the first draft was worked through directly — West Virginia's rate, Kentucky's premium‑cigar cut, Rhode Island's mail‑shipment statute, Arkansas's physical‑presence bar, New Jersey's tax bill, Massachusetts's cap question, and Kansas's licensing mechanics are now resolved (see each row above). Maryland's correction was the most consequential: it moved from the highest‑risk state in the project to one of the more favorable ones.

    The $10‑wholesale benchmark used to rank tax burden is a modeling convenience only — percentage, percentage‑with‑cap, and flat‑per‑cigar structures are normalized onto one axis by applying each state's formula to a hypothetical $10.00 wholesale‑price cigar. Recompute against each SKU's real invoice cost before pricing or remitting tax.

    Rates change often. Confirmed 2025–2026 changes include Maine (43%→75%), Illinois (36%→45%, further change in 2027), Ohio's inflation‑indexed cap ($0.65→$0.67), new remote‑seller laws in Nevada and Nebraska, a second SD license and a CO rate rise both effective 2027, Utah's DTC ban repeal effective 2027, and pending bills in DE, NJ, CT, TN, NY, and WV. Re‑pull this page shortly before any state‑by‑state launch.

    Efficacy scores are directional judgment calls, not a validated model — they weigh confirmed legality, licensing cost/complexity, tax burden, and rough population‑based market‑size potential for slow, zero‑paid‑advertising sales. They do not incorporate real sales data, competitor density, or logistics cost.
