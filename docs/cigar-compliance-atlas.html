    What's genuinely still open

    Four research passes on 2026‑09‑28 worked through nearly every "Needs Confirmation" flag by finding each state's specific delivery‑sale/mail‑order statute — the same pattern that already resolved AZ, OR, GA, IN, VA, WI, MO, and NC. 15 states flip to Legal, Illinois' current regime and Connecticut's current law are confirmed permissive, and Iowa flipped the other way — confirmed banned. The standout: Massachusetts requires no state license at all for a no‑premises internet retailer shipping directly to individual MA residents. A fourth pass then resolved Oklahoma — its cigar rate was corrected from $120/1,000 to the actual $90/1,000 (the original figure was cross‑contaminated from Louisiana's rate), and its shipping‑ban's "cigarette" definition (paper‑wrapped, ≤3 lbs/1,000) categorically excludes cigars. Only New Hampshire remains genuinely unresolved:

    1. New Hampshire — the only state with no resolution path found. No dedicated delivery‑sale/mail‑order statute exists (a likely regulatory gap, not a ban), but an Oct 2025 Liquor Commission wholesaler‑sourcing rule has unclear implications for a remote seller with no NH premises. A genuine "call the agency" item.
    2. New York — the operative "cigarette" definition within §1399‑LL itself is still not pinned down, though the pattern found in Oklahoma (a narrow, paper‑wrapping‑and‑weight‑based "cigarette" definition that excludes cigars) may well repeat here.
    3. Minnesota — the claimed $0.65/cigar inflation‑adjusted cap remains uncorroborated after two dedicated searches; rely on the statutory $0.50 figure.
    4. Maryland — core legality is resolved favorably, but the Remote Tobacco Seller License's bond requirement and MD Workers' Comp mechanics for an out‑of‑state business are unconfirmed. Contact ATCC (443‑300‑6990).
    5. Connecticut, Tennessee, Delaware — current law is confirmed favorable in all three, but each carries a live, named pending bill (HB 7275, SB 2086, HB 215); monitor rather than re‑research.
    6. General sales tax (all states) — this page covers tobacco excise tax and delivery‑sale licensing only. Oklahoma was confirmed to exempt tobacco products from its general sales tax once excise tax is paid; whether the other 48 states follow the same pattern, or whether standard economic‑nexus sales‑tax registration (post‑Wayfair, typically ~$100k in sales) applies on top, hasn't been separately verified per state.
    7. Utah, South Dakota, Nebraska, Illinois, Colorado — each has a confirmed, scheduled 2027 rule change. Build a 2027 compliance‑calendar review into any state‑entry plan.
    Methodology & limitations

    Research method. All 49 jurisdictions were researched via automated web search against primary government sources (state Departments of Revenue/Taxation, Comptrollers, ABC/tobacco‑enforcement agencies), cross‑checked across multiple queries per state. In several sessions, direct fetching of the underlying .gov pages was blocked by network policy, so those findings rely on search‑engine synthesis of primary sources rather than a full direct read. A compliance reviewer should open every cited source directly before relying on this page operationally.

    Aggregator errors caught and corrected: a claim that "Georgia bans internet tobacco sales" traced to law governing the country of Georgia, not the U.S. state, and was discarded; a Minnesota cap figure ($0.65) traced to what appears to be an Ohio source and remains unconfirmed after a second dedicated check; a North Carolina $400 permit‑fee claim could not be corroborated against the statutory $25 figure and was discarded in its favor; a West Virginia "51%" and "7%" rate claim were both superseded by the confirmed current 12% statute text; a Maryland "70% flat rate" claim was corrected to a three‑tier structure (30% general OTP / 15% premium cigars / 70% non‑premium cigars) once the actual bulletin was reviewed directly.

    Three research passes (all 2026‑09‑28): the first resolved the priority follow‑up list (Maryland, West Virginia, Kentucky's rate cut, Rhode Island, Arkansas, New Jersey's tax bill, Massachusetts's cap question, Kansas's licensing). A second and third pass then found each remaining state's specific delivery‑sale/mail‑order statute directly, resolving 17 of the last 20 "Needs Confirmation" flags (Florida, Alabama, Louisiana, Michigan, Minnesota, South Carolina, Kentucky, Tennessee, West Virginia, Mississippi, Wyoming, Delaware, New Jersey, Massachusetts, Maine, Illinois, and Connecticut's current law) — and flipped Iowa to confirmed Banned. Maryland's correction remains the single most consequential: it moved from the highest‑risk state in the project to one of the more favorable ones.

    The $10‑wholesale benchmark used to rank tax burden is a modeling convenience only — percentage, percentage‑with‑cap, and flat‑per‑cigar structures are normalized onto one axis by applying each state's formula to a hypothetical $10.00 wholesale‑price cigar. Recompute against each SKU's real invoice cost before pricing or remitting tax.

    Rates change often. Confirmed 2025–2026 changes include Maine (43%→75%), Illinois (36%→45%, further change in 2027), Ohio's inflation‑indexed cap ($0.65→$0.67), new remote‑seller laws in Nevada and Nebraska, a second SD license and a CO rate rise both effective 2027, Utah's DTC ban repeal effective 2027, and pending bills in DE, NJ, CT, TN, NY, and WV. Re‑pull this page shortly before any state‑by‑state launch.

    Efficacy scores are directional judgment calls, not a validated model — they weigh confirmed legality, licensing cost/complexity, tax burden, and rough population‑based market‑size potential for slow, zero‑paid‑advertising sales. They do not incorporate real sales data, competitor density, or logistics cost.
